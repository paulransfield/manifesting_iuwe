# 03 — senses and concept domains

**Status: design complete; 24-domain pilot and controlled sense catalogue under test.**

A word is not enough to identify a learning object. `bat` may refer to a flying animal, a cricket implement, or a baseball implement. ī-puāwai therefore separates a canonical word from its language-specific senses, and connects each sense to appropriate visual treatments, definitions, learner statements, and curriculum uses.

The operating hierarchy is:

```text
faculty → concept domain → referent concept → language-specific word sense
    → learner statement → visual/audio asset → practice event
```

Faculties remain broad social-learning contexts. The 24 concept domains provide the practical middle layer needed to organise pictionary-scale material. A source category helps locate an image; a concept domain organises learning; a referent concept holds intended meaning; a word sense gives that meaning language.

## Quality rule

> **Close is acceptable; wrong is not.**

A simple image may support a broad early-learning phrase, provided the phrase remains truthful to the visible referent. A visually impossible classification is not an inventive teaching opportunity; it is a reviewable mismatch.
