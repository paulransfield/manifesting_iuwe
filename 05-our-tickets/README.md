# 05. Our Tickets

> **A ticket is a visible invitation into a learning event—and a visible account of the relationships that make the event possible.**

## Why tickets matter

A community learning system needs more than content and goodwill. It needs a way to invite people, recognise contribution, sustain common capability, and show what happened without reducing every relationship to a transaction.

Our Tickets describes the bridge between a learning event and the economic or practical support around it: learner access, enabler contribution, koha, receipts, and accountable renewal. The aim is **collective advantage**—a system in which local community capacity and shared maintenance both have a legible place.

## The enabler relationship

An enabler may be a community trust, school, employer, health provider, creator, whānau member, or individual supporter. The system should record what the enabler is supporting and what evidence they may reasonably receive, without transferring ownership of the community or its learner data to them.

| Enabler may support | Enabler should not automatically receive |
|---|---|
| A scheduled learning event or series | Ownership of language or cultural authority |
| Local kaitiaki and review work | Identifiable learner data without a lawful, agreed basis |
| Community delivery infrastructure | The right to dictate local curriculum choices |
| Audio, media, and translation work | A licence beyond the agreed purpose |
| Shared platform maintenance | A claim over the commons or its future governance |

## Koha as structural coupling

The current design proposes a transparent 80:20 koha relationship: 80% is directed toward local community capacity, while 20% sustains shared operations such as infrastructure, curation, provenance, governance, and maintenance. This is a working operating model, not an unchangeable doctrine.

```text
agreed contribution
      ↓
traceable koha allocation
      ↓
local community capacity + shared operational capability
      ↓
visible delivery evidence, receipts, review, and renewal
```

The word **koha** is used deliberately. It names a relationship of giving, responsibility, and reciprocity. In implementation, the financial record still needs to be clear enough for an accountant, a community council, and an enabler to understand. The learning event itself should remain pedagogically clean; economic records belong in their related but separate lifecycle tables.

## A ticket lifecycle

| Stage | Question answered | Record created |
|---|---|---|
| **Invitation** | What is being offered, to whom, and under what conditions? | Access or delivery invitation. |
| **Contribution** | Who has enabled this event or programme? | Enabler and koha record. |
| **Allocation** | How is the support directed? | Transparent community/shared allocation. |
| **Delivery** | Did the agreed event occur? | Evidence and receipt link. |
| **Outcome review** | What learner engagement or community learning signal is appropriate to share? | Aggregated, consent-aware outcome evidence. |
| **Renewal / close** | Should the relationship continue, change, or conclude? | Review or closure record. |

## Privacy and dignity

A low-cost ticket must not become a route for extracting personal data from people with few alternatives. The system should collect the minimum information needed for access, consent, support, and accountability; explain why it is collected; and maintain clear routes to opt out or seek help.
