# 01. Our Seed-Banks

> **A seed-bank is not a word list. It is a community-held record of what may be sounded, named, practised, remembered, and renewed.**

## Why this chapter exists

Every learning pathway begins before a screen, a lesson, or a translation. It begins with sound, relation, and a community’s right to decide how its language is represented. Our Seed-Banks hold the small, reusable elements from which larger learning events can grow: phonemes, syllables, words, word senses, phrases, idioms, examples, and their community contexts.

The aim is not to flatten language into a universal vocabulary spreadsheet. The aim is to make local language capability **visible, reviewable, and reusable** without separating it from the people who give it meaning.

## The seed-bank chain

| Layer | What is held | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Phoneme** | A sound, its written representation, position, and teaching notes | Supports phoneme-first learning and language-specific pronunciation. |
| **Syllable / pattern** | Repeatable sound and letter patterns | Makes progression explicit rather than accidental. |
| **Word** | A canonical language form with identity and links | Keeps spelling, meaning, and curriculum use connected. |
| **Word sense** | One specific meaning of a word | Prevents `bat` the animal, `bat` the cricket implement, and `bat` the baseball implement being conflated. |
| **Phrase / idiom** | A meaningful unit beyond the isolated word | Returns language to relationship and ordinary use. |
| **Statement** | Short, cohort-appropriate learner language | Connects the sense to a speakable practice event. |

## The practical test

A seed-bank must survive real use. A second-language learner should be able to hear a sound, see a truthful visual where one is used, speak a short statement, and return to that item later without losing the connection to its meaning or source.

The ī-puāwai language bank is the current proof of this approach. It uses clean internal identities, controlled word senses, and links to visual, audio, practice, provenance, and community discussion rather than treating a filename as the meaning of an asset.

## Community authority

A seed-bank can be seeded from public language material, existing teaching resources, linguistic references, and locally created recordings. It becomes a community resource only through ongoing review. Communities should be able to state:

| Community decision | Repository consequence |
|---|---|
| What may be taught publicly | Visibility and rights settings are explicit. |
| What needs local explanation | Notes and examples remain linked to the sense. |
| What must not be simplified or detached | A review status prevents automatic release. |
| Who may approve changes | Kaitiaki and review roles are recorded. |
| How speakers and creators are acknowledged | Provenance and reciprocity records remain attached. |

## Working artefacts

This chapter will progressively link to phoneme inventories, seed-word sets, idiom lists, statement banks, pronunciation recordings, and community review protocols. The immediate implementation lives in [08. Our Ī-puāwai](../08-our-i-puawai/).

## The next small proof

The next useful proof is not an enormous dictionary. It is a reviewed chain from **one phoneme to one word sense to one short statement to one audio/visual practice event**. Once the chain is trustworthy, it can grow at the pace and under the authority of the community.
